How the numbers are calculated

Each support is a plywood template cut to the transition: a circle of radius R, plus vert above it. The supports stand in vertical planes that fan out evenly from the corner center, φ = corner angle ÷ sections apart. In a tight corner the profile starts at the corner center, so the surface is a sphere. In a wide corner the profile starts at the toe radius r₀, so the surface is a torus.

A rib whose lower skin-side edge sits at distance u from the corner center, measured in the support's plane (u = r₀ + R·sin a), spans the bay as a straight chord. Pinning both ends of that edge exactly to the support profile gives:

length = 2·u·sin(φ/2) − (o₁ + o₂)·cos(φ/2)

Here o is how far each support face is set off from its layout line: half the plywood thickness for a centered support, or zero for an outermost support whose face sits on the corner line.

The end cuts are the vertical faces of the supports. For a rib on a surface sloped at angle a, lying flat on the saw:

miter = atan(tan(φ/2)·sin a) bevel = asin(sin(φ/2)·cos a)

On edge, the roles swap: miter = atan(tan(φ/2)·cos a), bevel = asin(sin(φ/2)·sin a). a is taken at the middle of the rib's face.

Curved ribs. A rib is cut straight first, using the length and angles above, and then its skin face is cupped. Its skin face lies in the plane that contains the rib and the line perpendicular to the skin. The surface's curve in that plane has a radius equal to the distance along that perpendicular line from the skin to the corner's vertical axis:

cup radius ρ = R + r₀ / sin a cup depth = ρ − √(ρ² − (length/2)²)

For a sphere (r₀ = 0) this is just R for every rib. For a torus it grows toward the floor and equals the coping radius once the wall is vertical. The arc stays within a few thousandths of an inch of the true torus across a bay.
